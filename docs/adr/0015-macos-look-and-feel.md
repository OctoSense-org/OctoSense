# ADR 0015: A Mac's look and feel for the desktop and its apps

English | [简体中文](0015-macos-look-and-feel.zh-CN.md)

- Date: 2026-11-04
- Status: In progress; every part below is implemented on its branch. The final score and what stayed below target are recorded in the pull request.
- Builds on: [ADR 0004](0004-native-apps-hosting-and-peers.md), [ADR 0005](0005-app-contract.md), [ADR 0012](0012-app-host-api-discovery.md).

## Context

On a Mac, OctoSense read as a tablet shell with phone apps in windows. A first independent review scored the shell and five apps at 69 out of 100: controls sized for fingers, toolbars below a title bar, no menu bar that worked, card lists where a Mac has source lists, and phone wording.

The same apps ship on phones, and their phone layouts must not move. Script apps cannot be told apart at build time. Native apps, process apps and script apps each draw differently, and two of them, App Hub and OctosCode, live in their own repositories.

## Decision

### A Mac style is the default on a Mac

`DesktopStyle::Macos` and its dark twin are what a Mac starts in. Other styles stay available and unchanged. The Mac theme is data in Makepad's theme folders (`widgets/themes/macos`, `macos-dark`) plus shell chrome.

### Two gates, not guesses

Every style sheet states two values, in `crates/shell/src/octosense/style.rs::load_sheet`:

- `theme.ui_pointer`: true on every style that isn't a phone's. It decides a pointer layout.
- `theme.ui_unified`: true only on a Mac. It decides a unified toolbar.

An app with both shapes keeps two trees, `TouchRoot` and `DeskRoot`, chosen by `ui_pointer`. The touch tree stays byte for byte what phones had, which is how a phone is protected from this work. Sheets written in Rust pick a value with `by(touch, pointer)`.

### Unified windows

An opted-in app draws its own toolbar in the window's top band, 52 points high, with the traffic lights over it. No title bar is drawn. A press in the band that nothing in the app claims moves the window (`MpRunViewAction::TitlePress`). A process app is asked through the cursor it last reported; an in-process module through a real hit test.

### Menu bar, Dock, keyboard

- A Mac's menu bar replaces the top bar, with the app's name and File, Edit, View, Window and Help (`crates/shell/src/mac_menus.rs`).
- A Dock replaces the shelf. It hides with a window in full screen. Its icons and the bare desktop have right-click menus. Files stands first, as a Mac's Finder does, with Launchpad beside it. An app icon's menu has Options with Keep in Dock (checked while the app is kept) or, for a kept app that isn't running, Remove from Dock; the kept apps, in order, live in `dock-apps` in the shell's home, a new Mac's set until the person changes it. Files has no Options, because it never leaves. A hairline and a Trash always close it: the Trash opens Files on the Trash folder, and comes back to that window on a second press. Its menu has Open and Empty Trash, which asks in a Files window.
- A running app's Dock menu has Show All Windows: that app's windows on this desktop, side by side over the dimmed desk, as a Mac's App Exposé. The pointer, the arrows or Tab ring one and show its title, and a click or Return brings it forward. Full-screen and hidden windows are left out. Minimized ones sit in a row at its foot, under a faint rule, as a Mac's App Exposé shows them, and a click or Return brings one out of the Dock to the front. New Window shows only for the apps that open more than one window. Options has Open at Login, a tick per app kept in `login-apps` beside `dock-apps`: a desktop shell opens the ticked apps when it starts, in the order they were ticked, and a phone never does.
- The Trash shows papers while it holds something. Files says so with the wm_api request `TrashFull`, which the shell takes from Files alone, and Empty Trash is grey while the Trash is empty. Files asks before emptying it in Finder's compact alert, with its icon above the words.
- Notification Center fills the column down to the Dock and scrolls, with an overlay scroller. Empty, it says "No Notifications" and nothing more. A card's buttons show under the pointer, on the card the keyboard is on and while it is pressed, and keep their places at rest. At most three banners show at once; the rest wait their turn. A collapsed stack is its newest notification with the edges of two more under it. An open stack's header stands on the desk, with Show Less and a round × that reads Clear All under the pointer. A cleared notification brings no banner, and Command-Z brings it back. A notification a person opens leaves the column, as a Mac's does once clicked. Live cards, such as Mail's, a shipment's and the News digest, read as notifications, with their app's icon. The widgets are a Mac's small Calendar, which lists what is left of today from Calendar's own events, and an analog Clock. Edit Widgets, at the column's foot, takes a widget out or puts it back.
- Ask OctoSense and Ask <App> are as tall as what they hold, from 240 points up to the room above the Dock, and grow with a conversation. A reply shows a Copy button under the pointer, Shift-Command-C copies the latest reply, and a right click on a message opens a menu with Copy. An open shell menu draws over these panels in every style, phones included, because a Mac's status menus drop where Ask OctoSense floats.
- Window has Fill, Center and Move & Resize, as macOS 15's does. Fill and Move & Resize's halves and quarters use the window manager's snap zones, and Return to Previous Size brings the window's own place back. Center puts the window, at its own size, in the middle of the room above the Dock.
- The keymap is a Mac's: Cmd+W closes a tab in Terminal, Browser and Files and otherwise the window, Shift+Cmd+W always closes the window, Cmd+Space opens the search. The tiling chords don't apply, so Cmd-click and Cmd-scroll reach the app.
- Edit commands chosen with the pointer act on the field that had the keyboard before the menu opened.
- An app adds rows and menus to the bar with `fn app_menus()` and hears them in `fn on_menu_command(command)`. Native apps' rows are in `native_rows`; a native module can answer its own as they are now with `AppModule::menu_rows` (Weather, Notes and Reminders say Hide Sidebar or Show Sidebar, Calculator ticks the mode in use), and a process app sends its own with `makepad_wm_api::menu_rows` (`WmRequest::MenuRows`, the same rows as `app_menus()`); a chosen row comes back to it as `WmEvent::MenuCommand`, or as its key. A row may name a `submenu` of its menu: Task's View > Update Frequency. A row's Command key takes priority in that window, except the keys a text field edits with. A menu choice counts as a press for the services that need one. `apps/README.md` has the row's form.
- Tab moves between text fields, in reading order and going round, and selects what a single-line field holds. It stays among one script's fields, or one app's. Buttons and pop-ups are passed over, as on a Mac by default, in every style. `nav_group: true` keeps Tab inside a sheet; `keeps_tab: true` marks an editing surface it doesn't leave. Before this, Tab did nothing in the shell: a window's blur pass cut its controls off from the tree Tab walks.
- Network, Bluetooth, Sound and Battery in the menu bar open menus of the same family as the bar's. They say how things stand and end with a row to the Mac's own settings pane. The sound slider sets the Mac's sound off the UI thread. Sound lists the Mac's output devices under Output, the one in use ticked, and choosing one sends the Mac's sound there (CoreAudio, `crates/shell/src/mac_sound.rs`), as a Mac's Sound menu does; it has no Mute row, as a Mac's has none. Other styles keep their panels.
- Several desktops show as one status item with the current desktop's number. Its menu lists them, with their Control-number keys. Nothing sits between the mark and the menus. Other styles keep their numbers on the bar.
- A right click in a text field gives it the keyboard, selects the word under the pointer unless a selection is there already, and opens Cut, Copy, Paste and Select All. Rows with nothing to act on are grey. A sandboxed process app can't read the pasteboard, so it asks the shell (`AppToStudio::PasteClipboard`), and only on a person's choice.
- A person's drag stops at the size each app needs (`apps::mac_window_minimum`), before its toolbar collides or its first action falls off: 420 by 300 points for most, more for Calendar, Mail, App Hub and a few others. The app's own controls may still ask for less, and a window that is already smaller keeps what it has. Other styles keep their old floor.
- The system menu, in the Apple menu's place, has Recent Items and Force Quit…. Recent Items lists the last ten apps a person opened, newest first, kept in the shell's home, with Clear Menu. Force Quit… (Option-Command-Escape) lists the apps with windows, the one in front chosen, and asks on a sheet before it ends one: a process app's process is ended, and an in-process app's windows close without asking the app.
- The system menu holds the system's own rows, as the Apple menu does: About OctoSense, System Settings…, App Hub…, Software Update…, Recent Items, Force Quit… and Quit OctoSense…. Appearance and Desktop Style are in System Settings' Appearance (the desktop's menu keeps Appearance), Ask OctoSense… is in Help and on F8, and AI Providers and Approvals open from System Settings' Assistant.
- Help opens with a search field, as a Mac's does. What a person types lists the matching rows of the bar's other menus, a submenu's as "Appearance ▸ Dark", up to twelve, the first chosen so Return picks it; "No Menu Items" when none match, and Escape empties the field. Spotlight stays on Command-Space.
- Hide (Command-H) takes the app's windows off the desk and adds nothing to the Dock. Show All brings back what Hide put away and leaves a window a person minimized where it is. The app's Dock icon, its Window row, or naming it in Spotlight or Launchpad brings a hidden app back whole, and naming an app brings back its minimized window.
- On a Mac, an app that quits unexpectedly takes its window with it, and one alert says so, with Ignore and Reopen. In the other desktop styles its window stays, opaque, and says `<App> quit unexpectedly` with a way to reopen it. Raw exit details stay in the log.
- On a small screen, the bar gives way in order: status items first, then the clock's date, then the last menus, whose titles the arrow keys still reach. A menu taller than its room keeps its last rows under More. When the shell's window shrinks, a window wholly on the desk moves in, and gets smaller only if it must. That holds in every floating style. A window a person left partly off the desk stays where they left it.
- Control Center and Spotlight sit at the bar's right end beside the clock, in a Mac's order, and never give way on a narrow screen. Control Center drops a panel of modules: Network and Bluetooth tiles that say how each stands and open its details, Dark Mode, which turns the appearance over, and Sound with its slider. Nothing there switches the network or Bluetooth, which an app on the Mac can't do. The ✦ item stays on the bar whatever app is in front: it talks to that app's agent when it has one (Shift-F8), and otherwise opens Ask OctoSense (F8).
- Edit has Paste and Match Style (Option-Shift-Command-V) and Delete between Paste and Select All, and ends with Emoji & Symbols (Control-Command-Space), which opens the Mac's Character Viewer; Start Dictation… shows where AppKit answers it. Paste and Match Style pastes the pasteboard's text. An app with its own row of that name gets its own row, from the menu and from the keys.
- Help's search shows where a row it found lives: its menu opens at its title, a submenu too, with the row lit and a Mac's arrow pointing at it.
- Menus have one level of submenus. A context menu's row (`ContextRow::with_sub`) and a native app's menu bar row (`MenuRow::within`) open theirs beside them, by the pointer, the Right arrow or Return. A row with no title is a rule (`MenuRow::rule`), and `MenuRow::command` sends a named command that the module hears in `AppModule::menu_command`. A menu bar choice goes first to the native module in front, then to process apps and a script app's `on_menu_command`.
- Image, Browser and Sheets send their own menu bar rows, so the rows follow the window: Image's View and its Tools menu (Rotate Left, Rotate Right, Show Inspector), Browser's History, View and Bookmarks, grey where the window's own controls are, with History listing the last pages visited and Bookmarks the pages kept (Favorites in a submenu), as Safari's do, and Sheets' Format and Table menus.
- The desktop's right-click menu is Change Wallpaper… alone: OctoSense's desktop holds no files, so Finder's other rows would have nothing to act on. The appearance is chosen in System Settings and in Control Center, and Keyboard Shortcuts is in Help (Command-/).
- A classic title bar is 28 points, as macOS's has been since 11, and a hairline ends it over the window's content, except where Terminal's tab bar or its Settings window's toolbar carries on from it. The OctoSense style follows, since it mirrors a Mac's title bar.
- About OctoSense and a built-in app's About end with the copyright line from the repository's NOTICE; an App Hub app's About has none.
- Spotlight's System Settings panes wear the glyphs and colours of Settings' sidebar, and an app's preview lists Kind, Version and Where ("Comes with OctoSense" or "Installed from App Hub").

### Type

San Francisco leads the interface on a Mac, with its own italic and monospace faces. PingFang SC follows it for Chinese. This supersedes the request of 19 October 2026 to lead with PingFang for Latin text as well. Without PingFang, Chinese falls to the bundled face, as before. Other platforms' chains are unchanged.

### A press off a window

A press that leaves the focused window closes its open menu: the menu bar, the Dock, a classic title bar, the bare desktop. A process app hears `WmEvent::Focus` false then true. A module shares the shell's key focus, so the shell drops it and restores it. A script menu closes when the keyboard leaves its view. The field keeps the keyboard, so Edit > Paste still reaches it.

Inside a window, a left press off an open script menu only closes it, and what lies under the pointer doesn't hear it. A right press off it opens the menu of what it landed on.

A process app says when one of its menus is up (`AppToStudio::MenuUp`). While one is, a left press off its window only closes the menu, as a Mac spends the press that dismisses a menu: the app lets go of the keyboard, hears a press and its release far off its surface, and takes the keyboard back, so its field keeps it. The window takes the keyboard back at the focus message, not at that press: only a press inside a window gives it back. Terminal draws its own menus; they say they are up and close on that press too. A right press off the window closes the menu too, and goes on to open the menu of what it landed on.

### System Settings

Settings used to be long lists in the search panel. System Settings (`os.settings`, `apps/settings/bundle`) is a script app for the desktop styles, pinned last in a Mac's Dock, with General, Appearance, Assistant and Keyboard panes. It keeps one window, and a drag stops at 600 points wide, where its sidebar and search still fit.

- Its changes go through `settings.*` methods that only it may call (`crates/shell/src/app_settings_link.rs`). Each takes a press on its content, and none answers an agent.
- The search panel no longer changes settings. It finds a pane by its words and opens it, and it groups results under a Top Hit.
- Turning on the assistant's command execution takes the same typed words as before, on a sheet the host draws. The app can only ask for that sheet. The shell checks the words and makes the gesture in the one place it was already made ([ADR 0004](0004-native-apps-hosting-and-peers.md), section 12).
- Theme is listed only in the style that draws it. Desktop Picture is listed in the styles that draw one. In Omarchy it moves to the theme's next picture. On a Mac it is a choice of Abyssal Currents, which changes with the appearance, Tokyo Night, or one of four colours. It is kept in the shell's home, and the desktop's menu reaches it with Change Wallpaper…, as a Mac's does.
- Sleep, Restart, Lock and Log Out stay with the Mac, because OctoSense is an app on it.

### What script apps gain

These are public and apply in every style. They describe what an app does and don't widen what it may reach ([ADR 0012](0012-app-host-api-discovery.md), rule 12 in `AGENTS.md`):

- Native menus on any `GestureView`: `menu`, `on_menu`, `menu_urls`, `open_menu()`, `menu_current`.
- `clipboard.write`: write only, up to 1 MiB, after a press on the app's content in the last 5 seconds, and never from an agent.
- `settings.open`: opens AI Providers and nothing else, after a press on the app's content, so a "no model" dead end has a way out.
- `files.pick_photo` with `full_size`, and `files.export` with `bundled`: an app's own art, including art compiled into the shell.
- The keyboard: `focus()` on a `TextInput` and its `on_escape`; `on_key` and `focus()` on a `GestureView`, and Command and Shift on `on_tap`; `scroll_y()` and `set_scroll_y(y)` on a scroll view. A press on a button, a check box or a radio button leaves the keyboard with a view that holds it through `on_key`.
- The shared `UiSearchClear`, and a Mac activity indicator in `LoadingSpinner`.
- The shared `UiAlertIcon` and `ui_alert_icon`: the app's icon in its style's frame, drawn by the shell (`ReviewAppIcon`, which every Splash isolate reaches). A host without the shell has none, and `ui_alert_icon` is false there.
- A field that stands for a whole form's takes Tab with `on_tab`. `nav_group` and `keeps_tab` say where Tab stops.
- `open_embed` and `on_ready` on a `WebReader`, and `over_web: true` on a panel drawn over one.

### Web views

A web view is the platform's own and lies over everything its window draws. On a Mac, its host layer has a bitmap mask that cuts out what the shell, a menu, a sheet or a panel with `over_web` draws in front, and the cut parts take no clicks. Other platforms hide the view while something overlaps it. Phones don't count covers, so a phone's page is as it was. While a page is open on a desktop, every frame walks the whole widget tree to find those covers.

### Presses and redraws

With a mouse, a `render()` waits while the widget it would replace holds the press or owns an open menu, and a pending result draws before a press lands. A click no longer vanishes because a result arrived between press and release. Touch is unchanged.

A view's `finger_down` and `finger_up` actions are the primary button's or a touch's, in every style. A right press on a row isn't a click on it: a context menu reads the raw mouse event instead.

### At rest

A process app's clock slows while the app is quiet. The host beats every 8 ms while anything happens, and every 50 ms once the app has had no input and shown no new frame for a quarter of a second. Each beat wakes every widget in the shell, so thirteen open apps at rest cost a fifth of what they did. This holds in every style.

### File panels

A process app has no window of its own to hang a panel from, and the studio runtime used to drop its `SelectFileDialog`. On a Mac it now asks the shell (`AppToStudio::OpenPanel`), and the shell puts up the Mac's own panel if that app's window is in front. The shell always answers (`StudioToApp::OpenPanelAnswer`), and a refusal reads as a cancel. Image, PDF and Finance open files this way. On other systems they keep their own Open sheet, and a test on a Mac sees that sheet by passing `--open-home=<dir>`. A hidden shell answers the panel from `MAKEPAD_TEST_OPEN_FILE`, so the real panel is **unverified**.

### A Mac's own panels for a sandboxed app

On a Mac, Sheets opens and saves through the Mac's own panels, anywhere the person chooses. This is a behaviour change: Sheets used to offer only its own sheets, tied to its own folder. Its sandbox doesn't change. Its manifest still asks for nothing outside its folder, and nothing here widens it.

- A process app asks the shell for a save panel the way it asks for an open panel (`AppToStudio::SavePanel`, answered by `StudioToApp::SavePanelAnswer`), on the same terms: only the window in front gets one, and a refusal reads as a cancel. Sheets' panels start in its own folder.
- When the shell's own panel answers a process, the shell records a grant: that process's connection and the exact file the person picked, as it really is at that moment. Links are followed, and a file that isn't there yet is placed by its folder's real path. The app then asks the shell to read or replace that file (`AppToStudio::ChosenFile`, answered by `StudioToApp::ChosenFileAnswer`). Any other path, another file in the same folder, the folder itself and another process's file are refused with a plain error. Nothing is listed or deleted.
- The shell replaces a file whole. It writes a hidden file beside it and then moves that file over it, so a failed write never cuts the person's file short. Reads and writes stop at 64 MiB, and the work runs off the shell's UI thread.
- The work never trusts the path again, because the chosen file may sit in a folder the app can change. On Unix a read opens the file without following a link and reads only when the opened file's own path (`F_GETPATH` on a Mac) is the chosen one; a write opens the file's folder the same way and makes, and moves, the new file inside that open folder. A link or a moved folder put in the way after the check is refused.
- Grants live only in the shell's memory, for that process's connection. A restart, a reconnect or the end of the process drops them, and none is written to disk.
- A file saved in the app's own folder stays one of its documents: the app reaches it itself, and the next window reopens it. A last document outside that folder isn't reopened, and the next window starts on a new workbook. This is a known gap.
- Without a Mac panel (other styles, phones, in-process hosting, other systems), Sheets keeps its own Open and Save sheets as they were. Its own alerts say why an open or a save was refused. `--app-sheets` shows those sheets on a Mac, for tests.
- A hidden shell answers the save panel from `MAKEPAD_TEST_SAVE_DIR` and the open panel from `MAKEPAD_TEST_OPEN_FILE`, so the real panels are **unverified**.

### Windows that remember

Three requests let a process app shape its own window. `WmRequest::Resize` sizes the content of the asking process's own window. It works only while that window floats on a desktop style, and the result is clamped to the visible screen and a minimum size. `WmRequest::LaunchOrFocus` makes Launch's checks and nothing wider. It brings forward the app's window started with exactly those arguments, or launches one. `WmRequest::FrameAutosave` names a frame, kept under the app's id and that name in the shell's own state under `OCTOSENSE_HOME`. The shell saves the frame when the window closes or the shell quits, and puts it back on a visible display when the app names it again.

Terminal uses all three. On a Mac, Command-, opens one Settings window with toolbar panes instead of a modal sheet, and a phone keeps its sheet. Its panes are General and Profiles, as Terminal's are. General holds what isn't a profile's: the profile new windows and tabs open with, their folder, the tab bar and other programs' access. Profiles lists the profiles with the default marked, has +, − and a ⋯ menu under the list, and shows Text, Window, Shell, Keyboard and Advanced as tabs for the chosen profile. The live settings file is the default profile and each saved profile keeps its own file, so files written before still load. A window's frame comes back with it. A Terminal opened after Command-Q brings back the windows it had, each tab in its folder and showing what it showed, as plain text above a new shell, as Terminal's Restore text when reopening windows does (on by default, on a profile's Window tab). The text is kept beside the window's file, readable only by its owner, and taken once. Colours, running jobs and split panes don't come back, and closing a window forgets it and its text. Command-Q with only idle tabs quits without asking; a program still running asks first.

### Small things a Mac does

- **An app that quits unexpectedly.** On a Mac its window closes with it, whether it ran in its own process or in the shell, and one alert says "<App> quit unexpectedly." with Ignore and the blue default Reopen. Reopen opens a new window where the old one was; Return reopens and Escape ignores. One alert shows at a time and an app has at most one; the rest wait (`crates/shell/src/quit_alert.rs`). No banner repeats it. The other desktop styles keep the stopped window, with its icon, those words and a blue Reopen, and there a process app reopens only from that button.
- **A window that can't grow.** `apps::mac_window_fixed` (Calculator and Reference) greys the green light, Zoom and Enter Full Screen in macOS style. Its edges don't size it either; its own controls still ask for room.
- **Edit rows follow the field.** A focused field reports `HostedEdit` (`makepad_edit` on the hosted channel): whether it has a selection, text, undo and redo. The rows go grey from that report. `field: false` means no field is saying, and the rows stay live for an editor of an app's own. A button, checkbox or pop-up holding the keyboard (`CxDraw::key_focus_is_control`) has no text, as on a Mac: the app's own Undo, Copy or Select All acts then. Neither has a view that got the keyboard only because it was pressed, such as a canvas, a list or a segment. makepad marks the keyboard a press gives a control or a view (`Cx::set_key_focus_on_press`, `Cx::key_focus_pressed`) and keeps the mark across a sheet. There the app's own Edit rows take their keys and stand in the Edit menu. A key the app has no row for still reaches the control or the view.
- **Ticks have a column.** A menu with any ticked row keeps a column for ticks, so the other rows line up. In an app's menus on a Mac a row's words start 19 points in, clear of the ✓. A pop-up's menu hangs left by what its rows keep (`PopupMenu::words_inset`), and a `DropDown` with `popup_words_over` puts the current row's words over its own. A `GestureView` pop-up does the same over its face when told where the face's words start (`menu_words_at`, 10 for `UiPopup`).
- **Find on the page.** Writer's Edit menu has Find… (⌘F), Find Next (⌘G) and Find Previous (⇧⌘G). Find… shows a bar under the format bar; the match is selected on the page as the words are typed, case aside, and Return and the bar's arrows go on round the document with the keyboard still in the field. Escape or Done puts the bar away and gives the page the keyboard on the match (`DocumentEditor` script methods `find` and `find_done`, `draft::find_all`).
- **Writer's outline wraps.** A long heading in the outline takes a second line, as Pages' table of contents wraps one, so headings that share their first words still read apart.
- **Approvals is a sheet.** On a Mac it has Done as its blue default and no window lights; Escape and Return do what Done does. It is one of System Settings' pages and hangs from under that window's toolbar: opened from anywhere else, such as the system menu or a chat's Open Settings, it brings System Settings forward at its Assistant pane first. Its time box chooses an app from a pull-down that drops below its button. Rules read as short titles, such as "Mail may send messages", with their state under them, one button each and an ⓘ that opens the rule's details on a sheet over it. Turn Every Rule Off sits under the list, where a Mac puts Remove All. What each press records is unchanged. A phone keeps its scrim.
- **The caret stays in view.** A scrolling pane follows a focused field's caret on desktop, in every style (`FollowCaret`).
- **Long menus.** A script menu too tall for its window shows arrows at its ends, in every style. Hovering one scrolls, and so does the wheel.
- **Sheets hang, with one dim.** A sheet hangs flush from the foot of the toolbar, or of the bars under it, square where it hangs. Host sheets, script apps' sheets (`ui_sheet_dim`) and the native apps' sheets dim the window behind them with the same black: 14% in light and 32% in dark.
- **A right click rings what it opened on.** Mac themes turn on `PopupMenu.context_ring`. A right press on a view up to 320 points tall rings it while its menu is open. A left press never does.
- **An app's own Command-W.** A script app's enabled row with `key: "cmd+W"` takes the key. File's Close Window then says Shift-Command-W.
- **Destructive alerts.** Cancel is blue and last, so Return can't delete.
- **An alert shows whose it is.** On a Mac most apps' alerts are a Mac's compact alert: 260 points wide, the app's 64-point icon on top, the title in bold and the words centred under it. Two buttons sit side by side, as wide as each other, with the default on the right; three, or two whose words don't fit half the alert, stand one over another with the default on top. As in AppKit's, no word stands alone on the last line. Calculator, Clock, Notes, Reminders, Weather, Files, Finance, Mail, News, Photos, Calendar, PDF Tools, Quick Deck, Writer, AI Providers, YouTube and OctosCode draw it, and Mail's review of a message to send is the same alert at 300 points, with the exact message in a box that scrolls. Sheets', Task's and Terminal's alerts keep the app's 52-point icon beside their words. A form on a sheet has no icon.
- **A sheet owns the keyboard.** `Button{key_default: true}` answers Return and `Button{key_cancel: true}` answers Escape while it shows. While one shows, nothing under its sheet hears a key, and what had the keyboard has it again when the sheet leaves, unless someone has asked for it since. The hold belongs to the app's window (`MpModuleView`), so a sheet in a window behind never takes the keyboard from the window in front. Of stacked sheets, the frontmost answers. Return or Escape on such a sheet counts as a press, as a click on the button would. A field on the sheet still takes the keyboard when pressed or given `focus()`, and keeps its `on_return`. A question with a destructive answer gives both marks to its blue Cancel. A script sheet keeps its own draw list, so most frames don't draw its buttons: the view that keeps the list says they still show (`KeyButtons`). A hidden view hands keys on, but a button in it doesn't answer them. While such a sheet shows, the app's own rows in the menu bar are grey, and Close Window and Command-W wait for it, as on a Mac. A key from a menu row sends its key up too, so a process app doesn't go on holding its modifiers.
- **Popovers hang from what opened them.** Calendar's new event, Reminders' Details and Weather's Add Location hang from their buttons with a beak. A press off Reminders' Details keeps what was typed. With no room beside the button, or a question to ask, they stay a sheet.

The assistant's tools read as what they do, such as "look things up", from each tool's reviewed risk and whether the shell asks first, never as tool ids. ### How a look is judged

`tools/app-ux/macos-rubric.json` adds Mac-specific dimensions to the acceptance rubric, and `tools/app-ux/macos_score.py` totals a card. Any of these fails a surface whatever its score: a failed core journey, an essential control clipped at default size, a blank dead end, developer text in normal use, a script error or a panic. A required state nobody looked at caps its dimension at 0.8. An author's own card is not acceptance, and a desktop phone preview never stands in for a phone.

### What doesn't change

- Secrets stay on host-owned sheets, and approvals stay the host's. On a Mac they read as sheets, and Return deliberately doesn't approve, because a sheet can arrive while someone is typing.
- The sandbox profiles stay as they were. Apps with no network rights keep none, including under test.
- What approvals record is unchanged. Only the words shown on a Mac are.

## Delivery

Makepad's part is one more stacked runtime patch. App Hub and OctosCode land in their own repositories first, and this repository's pins follow them. No `[patch]` or path outside `.sources/` ships.

## Consequences

- An app author writes two trees when a screen's shape differs. `apps/README.md` lists the traps: a body `if … else` that ends differently removes the enclosing view, a `Fill` child in a `Fit` parent collapses, and a script `if`/`else` statement whose first block ends in a statement that leaves no value (a loop, a `let`, or one ending in `;`) while its `else` block ends in an expression stops the function at its next statement ("pop_stack_value on empty stack"). The last is a Splash fault, kept as a minimal case for Makepad.
- Numbered lists in Markdown and HTML text keep one indent per nesting level, with each number right-aligned before its words (`TextFlow::begin_ordered_list_item`). This reaches every style and the phones: Mail, Notes and Browser's reader draw their lists through it.
- A widget's `visible` is state that a style's reapply keeps (`#[visible]` fields survive `Apply::ScriptReapply`). A process app builds its tree before the shell's style arrives, so a `visible:` that depends on the style keeps its first value. Such an app sets it from Rust when the style is applied, as Browser does for its menu tool.
- Framework fixes made on the way reach every style: stale regex captures, a first connection refused to sandboxed apps on macOS 14, a stuck cursor over scroll views, a resize race in process apps, hyphens and currency signs in line breaking, and a panic when a YouTube results page put a character across the end of a parse window.
- The menu-closing rule means a process app hears a focus change for a press it never saw. Hosted windows act on it, and a custom menu in an app can close on it.
- Menu changes reach every style. A `DropDown`'s menu stays inside the window and covers its button. A menu row's tick takes the highlight colour only while the row is lit. A pop-up asked for by its view slides back at the right edge, and only a menu at the pointer flips to its left. A menu taller than its room scrolls with the wheel inside that room, opening with its current row in view. It shows an arrow at each cut end, and hovering one scrolls.
- A process app can ask for the pasteboard's text (`AppToStudio::PasteClipboard`) and hears `WmEvent::Quitting` before the shell closes it.
- A Mac's menu bar is the only place an app's `app_menus()` rows and their keys reach, so a command an app moves there must stay on screen in the other styles. Quick Deck keeps Undo and Redo in its toolbar off a Mac for this reason. Two known gaps remain: PDF Tools keeps one tree for every style, so its phone layout changed too (the floating page bar became the toolbar's page and zoom tools), and its Fit Width and Fit Page are now in a Mac's View menu and Read mode's bar, so a phone or another desktop reaches them only in Read mode; and when AI Providers can't read its saved models, a desktop other than a Mac shows Try Again but no Add Model… until a load succeeds.

- Shared widgets gained submenus (`ContextRow::with_sub`), ticks (`ContextRow::ticked`) and a menu's placed position (`PopupMenu::placed_at`) in context menus; `MenuRow::within`, `rule` and `command` with `AppModule::menu_command`; `Image.quarter_turns`; MapView's `mac_look`; and the disk map's `group_bands`. Each is off or unused until asked for, so other apps draw as before.
- The shell says which style its own process draws (`desktop_style::set_process_style`), for the Mac style only, so in-process apps take the Mac's pointer and menu conventions on every run. The OctoSense style still takes its answer by chance in process. Making it follow its own sheet changes that style, so it is left to the maintainers.
- Stored formats grow. Sheets saves its two Currency forms as the format codes `m` and `n`, which an older Sheets reads as damaged. Browser's bookmarks gain optional `folder` and `note`. AI Chat saves its model choice in the app's Makepad home. Notification Center keeps `glance-widgets-off` in the shell's home.
- Left, each for a reason given in the pull request: Clock's ring as a notification with Snooze and Stop, Notes' and Reminders' Print and Export, Calculator's Programmer mode, Numbers' header rows and freezing, Maps' Driving, Transit and Satellite, Quick Look and sending for Mail's attachments, a palette scoped to OctosCode's conversation, Image's Copy of a picture, PDF's page turns and display modes, Browser's Download Linked File, Photos' floating Info and Option-click Rotate, and Task's Energy tab.

## Not verified by this decision's work

- Physical phones. The touch trees were compared in desktop previews only.
- A real model, real mail servers beyond a scripted one, and the real macOS save panel. Hidden runs use a test answer.
- Control-click as a right click. Hidden runs bypass the code path.
- The real open and folder panels. Hidden runs use a test answer, as for the save panel.
- A Mac set to Dark or Auto. The test Macs are Light, and a hidden shell doesn't follow the Mac's appearance.
- A real laptop's battery. Hidden runs use a stand-in for `pmset`.
- The Mac's real System Settings panes opening from a status menu, and the Mac's real sound changing.
- A masked web view on a real screen: real clicks, hover and cursor in cut parts. Other platforms' hide-while-overlapped path.
- A trackpad's two-finger click on an assistant's message or an OctosCode list row. Hidden runs press the instrument's secondary button.
- App Hub's reasons other than "Requires a newer version of OctoSense", and the tool words for tools that change things or ask first. Unit tests only; no rig catalog reaches them. A time zone east of UTC on screen, likewise.
- App Hub's Update All with more than one real update, and a fresh public catalog: the rig's catalog offers one update, and the public catalog was past its freshness limit, so every Get there waited for a newer one.
- Terminal's restored text after the Mac itself restarts or logs out. Hidden runs quit Terminal and open it again.
- In Terminal's Profiles: Duplicate, Restore Defaults, a rename that changes only the case, and scrolling a long list of profiles. The rig doesn't run them.
- Emoji & Symbols bringing up the Character Viewer. A hidden run can't show a system window.
- Files' This Mac search and Columns view on a real disk. The rigs browse the sample home, which lists at once.
- Task's Idle Wake Ups on an Intel Mac, and the pressure graph's yellow and red.
- Terminal with a profile that names a font other than the default: its first window may still start the shell at the bundled font's grid.
- YouTube playing a video on macOS 15 or older. A hidden window paints a playing video only on macOS 26, so playback was checked only there.
